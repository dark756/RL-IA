CLAUDE'S OBSERVATIONS
=====================

A review of "my observations.txt" (in this folder), written on 2026-10-13 after the study finished.
Each section takes one of your points, says what holds and what doesn't, and suggests a wording.

Sources
- results/test_results.csv, results/summary.csv, results/peaks.csv and the charts in plots/.
- Two things were worked out for this review and aren't saved in results/:
  - the importance shares recomputed without the unstable PPO corner, with variance_shares() from plots.py;
  - estimated minutes of training to reach each stage. Each run's total time ("seconds" in its state file)
    is split by the share of its steps done by that stage. Each run had one CPU thread, with 11 runs at once.

Terms used below
- return: a model's mean over the 300 test landings, averaged over the 3 seeds.
- landed %: the share of test landings where the lander came to rest.
- high lr: 1e-3 or 3e-3. Low lr: 1e-4 or 3e-4.
- the unstable PPO corner: PPO with lr 1e-3 or 3e-3 on a 64x64 or 256x256 network. That's 4 settings and
  72 models.


IN SHORT
--------
- Point 1 is mostly produced by the unstable PPO corner. Without it, training episodes explain 49% of the
  spread between models, and settings combined only 15%.
- Point 2 holds for heavy wind, but low gravity goes the other way.
- Points 3 and 4 are right about PPO. The rule is a cut-off rather than a match of lr to size, and the
  "most efficient" claim depends on what efficiency means.
- In point 5, "definitely" is too strong: the best PPO setting only ties the average SAC.
- The notes don't yet answer the second half of the study question: where performance drops off.


1) "What mattered most is the combined effect of the settings"
--------------------------------------------------------------
Partly right, but misleading.

The 45% "settings combined" share comes mostly from the unstable PPO corner. Those 4 settings are only 17%
of the models, but they also cause 72% of the "seed (luck)" share. The same analysis without them:

                         All 432 models    Without the unstable corner (360)
  training episodes           12%                    49%
  network size                 1%                    10%
  algorithm                   12%                     7%
  learning rate                7%                     0.4%
  settings combined           45%                    15%
  seed (luck)                 23%                    18%

"Settings combined" adds up 7 combination bars, so ranking it against single settings isn't a fair
comparison.

Suggested wording: "Training episodes matter most. The settings only interact strongly in one corner: PPO
with lr 1e-3 or higher on a 64x64 or 256x256 network becomes unstable. That corner produces most of the
'combined' share and most of the seed luck."


2) "Adverse conditions reward the correct algorithm and training episodes above all else"
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This holds for heavy wind only.

Heavy wind supports it.
- The algorithm's share rises from 9% (ideal) to 17%.
- The gap in landed % between the best PPO setting (256x256, lr 1e-4) and SAC 64x64/256x256 at 4000
  episodes grows from 6 points in ideal (93% vs 99%) to 15 points in heavy wind (81% vs 96%).

Low gravity goes the other way.
- The training-episodes share falls to 4%, and seed luck rises to 29%.
- The best PPO setting lands 74% of the time here, and SAC 64x64/256x256 lands 73%: the gap disappears.
- SAC runs out of time while hovering in 16% of its low-gravity landings.
- Extra training helps less: from 125 to 4000 episodes SAC gains 201 points here, against 263 in ideal.

Heavy gravity looks just like ideal (algorithm 9%, training episodes 15%). It isn't really adverse:
training gravity goes down to -11.9, and 14% of training landings fall between -11 and -11.9.

"Above all else" doesn't hold: in every column, settings combined and seed luck are bigger than both the
algorithm and training episodes.

Comparing shares across columns is risky. Each share is a fraction of that condition's own spread, and low
gravity's spread is about twice the others' (variance 106k against roughly 50k). Compare returns or
landed % instead.

Suggested wording: "Heavy wind is where SAC's lead grows. Low gravity, which training never sees, is the
hardest condition for every model, and extra training helps least there."


3) Learning rate per network size
---------------------------------
PPO 16x16, "high lr works well with a small network":
- High lr learns faster: at 500 episodes lr 1e-3 is at 126 and lr 1e-4 at -277.
- By 4000 episodes all four lrs sit between 112 and 161, and their seeds overlap (lr 1e-3: 153 to 172,
  lr 1e-4: 88 to 169). The final order is within luck.
- PPO 16x16 never reaches 200 at any lr.

PPO 64x64, "low to medium lr works well":
- Low lr wins. lr 1e-4 reaches 209 and is the only 64x64 setting to cross 200. lr 3e-4, SB3's default,
  reaches 175.
- lr 1e-3 peaks at 136 at 1000 episodes, then sinks to 83.

PPO 256x256, "low lr works best": correct. lr 1e-4 reaches 220 and lr 3e-4 reaches 212.

A sharper PPO rule: it's a cut-off, not a gradual match of lr to size. lr 1e-3 or higher breaks PPO on any
network bigger than 16x16, and 64x64 and 256x256 share the same safe limit of 3e-4 or lower.

SAC, "models converge with episodes, no trend among lrs":
- True for 64x64 and 256x256: at 4000 episodes every lr ends between 234 and 275.
- Not true for 16x16. It ranges from 78 to 201 and is still improving at 4000. 38% of its training
  landings hover until the 1000-step limit.
- At 16x16 the seed matters more than the lr. With lr 1e-3 the three seeds end at 21, -9 and 222.


4) Return against network size
------------------------------
"PPO needs a low lr if a big network is used": correct. The timing is worth adding.
- 256x256 at lr 1e-3 looks good up to 2000 episodes: return 169, 72% landed.
- It then collapses to -355 at 4000, with 4% landed.
- A short test would have picked this setting. This is the drop-off the study question asks about.

"Most efficient was PPO lr 1e-4 at 1000 episodes": say it's 256x256, since 16x16 at that lr and stage only
reaches 42. Then say what "efficient" means, because the answer depends on it:
- Per minute of CPU, it holds. It reaches 179 and lands 73% in about 17 minutes. SAC 64x64 at lr 3e-3 needs
  about 24 minutes to reach 160 (at 250 episodes), and SAC 256x256 at lr 3e-3 about 90 minutes to reach
  196 (at 500).
- Per environment step, it's the other way round. PPO used 538k steps. SAC 64x64 at lr 3e-3 reached 160 in
  120k steps and 206 in 216k.
- Per training episode, SAC wins too. At 500 episodes SAC 64x64 at lr 3e-3 is at 206, and the best PPO
  setting at 126.
- The minutes are estimates (see Sources).

"High lr early on may go wrong for SAC with bigger networks": this rests on one seed.
- With SAC 256x256 at lr 3e-3, seed 1 was at -405 after 125 episodes and -563 after 250. Seeds 0 and 2
  were at 43 and 22 after 125 episodes, and 139 and 127 after 250.
- All three recover by 500 episodes, ending at 185 to 202.
- On 64x64, lr 3e-3 is the best SAC setting at 250 episodes (160).

Suggested wording: "High lr makes early SAC less predictable: one seed in three crashed hard, then
recovered."


5) "SAC is better in general, but a well-configured PPO could definitely beat the average SAC"
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
At best a tie, so "definitely" is too strong.
- At 4000 episodes, the best PPO setting (256x256, lr 1e-4) has return 220 and lands 85%. The average SAC
  setting has return 218 and lands 82%.
- SAC 16x16 drags that average down. Every SAC 64x64 and 256x256 setting beats the best PPO setting; the
  lowest of them is at 234.
- PPO's real advantage is time. At 4000 episodes the best PPO setting took about 54 minutes on one CPU
  thread, against about 9 hours for SAC 256x256. SAC used half the steps (0.89M against 1.77M).
- Per minute they're roughly even: PPO 256x256 at lr 1e-4 reaches 201 in about 34 minutes, and SAC 64x64
  at lr 3e-3 reaches 206 in about 43 minutes.

Suggested wording: "SAC beats PPO at the same number of training episodes, and only SAC 16x16 loses to a
well-tuned PPO. PPO gets through episodes about 7 to 13 times faster, so for the same training time the
two are roughly even."


WHAT THE NOTES DON'T COVER YET
------------------------------
The drop-off half of the study question.
- Only PPO drops off, and only in the unstable corner:
  - 64x64 peaks at 250 episodes with lr 3e-3, and at 1000 with lr 1e-3;
  - 256x256 at lr 1e-3 peaks at 2000;
  - 256x256 at lr 3e-3 never learns: it ends at -968 and lands none.
- SAC's only dips are 4 and 20 points, on 64x64 with lr 1e-3 and 3e-3 after 2000 episodes.
- So more training never hurt SAC up to 4000 episodes. It does hurt PPO when the lr is too high.

Seed luck is the biggest single bar in chart 1 (23%). With 3 seeds, differences smaller than the bands in
chart 3 aren't reliable.

Chart 5 shows different failure modes. Early SAC models run out of time while hovering (up to about 45% of
a condition's landings). PPO models crash instead.

Low gravity is the hardest condition, and the only one outside the training range. SAC lands 61% there,
against 80-90% everywhere else, so it tests how well the models handle planets they haven't seen.

The 1% for network size doesn't mean size doesn't matter.
- Outside the unstable corner, 16x16 is the weakest network for both algorithms.
- The share looks small because size cuts both ways: in the unstable corner a bigger network is worse,
  and everywhere else it's better.
- Without that corner, size explains 10%.

Say which measure you're using. Ranked by landed % instead of mean return, training episodes explain 40%
of the spread, against 12%.

Give concrete values for vague words such as "high/low lr", "efficient" and "converge". Small typos:
"generasl", "definately".
